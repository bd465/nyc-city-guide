Getting Around the Subway

New York City has an extensive subway system that runs 24 hours a day and is the fastest way to get around. Popular subway lines include the 1, 2, and 3 trains on the West Side of Manhattan, and the 4, 5, and 6 trains on the East Side. The A, C, and E lines are useful for traveling between Midtown and Lower Manhattan, while the L train connects Manhattan to Williamsburg in Brooklyn. Subway stations are labeled by direction, such as Uptown and Downtown. It is important to check the correct direction before entering. Express trains skip certain stops, while local trains stop at every station, so reading signs on the platform is essential.

Walking the City

Walking is one of the best ways to explore New York City, especially in areas like SoHo, Greenwich Village, and Central Park. Streets in Manhattan are organized in a grid system, making it easier to navigate. Avenues run north to south, while streets run east to west.

Airports and Getting to Manhattan

New York City is served by three major airports: JFK, LaGuardia, and Newark. From JFK, visitors can take the AirTrain and connect to the subway or Long Island Rail Road. From Newark, travelers can take NJ Transit into Penn Station. Taxis and ride-shares are also available but are usually more expensive.

Payment and Budget Tips

Most places in New York City accept credit and debit cards, and contactless payment is widely used. However, it is still helpful to carry some cash for small purchases, street vendors, or tips. Sales tax is added at checkout, and tipping 15 to 20 percent is expected at restaurants.

Be Prepared

New York City is generally safe, especially in busy areas, but it is important to stay aware of your surroundings. Keep personal belongings secure in crowded places like Times Square or the subway. The subway can be crowded during rush hours, typically between 7 to 10 AM and 4 to 7 PM on weekdays. Traveling outside of these times can make your trip more comfortable.
